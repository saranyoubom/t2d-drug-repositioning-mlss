## 2. What the MLSS is in this dataset (code, port, `mlss_facts.txt`)
- **Steps of the R code that its header and the Methods do not state:** (i) a drug enters only if a DrugCentral row has GENE equal to a hub gene (exact string after upper-casing) and the target organism is *Homo sapiens*; (ii) the pIC50 is the largest ACT_VALUE among human rows of type IC50, Ki or EC50 with RELATION "="; (iii) the correlation lookup is the Hes1-normalized pooled Pearson matrix (all 120 pairs significant, smallest *r* 0.752, none negative), used for V, Synergy and Antagonism whatever the consensus status; (iv) ties keep the original row order.
- **Drugs.** Of the four hubs only GLP1R and CACNA1D have DrugCentral rows (TCF7L2, PTBP1 and WNT5A have none, in any organism). 18 drugs: 7 on GLP1R, 11 on CACNA1D, each with one hub target. 153 = 77 cross-target + 55 (CACNA1D x CACNA1D) + 21 (GLP1R x GLP1R).
- **Structure of the score.** Cross-target pairs: C = B = V = 1, ranks 1-77, MLSS 7.65 to 9.96. Same-target pairs: C = 0, B = 1, V = 0, ranks 78-153, MLSS 2.70 to 4.05. Antagonism is 0 for all. Synergy > 0 for 30 combinations (0.747 to 0.960), all through the single correlation Cacna1d-Glp1r (0.960); 47 cross pairs have 0 because a drug lacks a pIC50. 47 distinct scores among 153; 125 combinations share a score with another (28 at 2.700, 14 at 7.650, 12 at 2.754, 10 at 4.050; top tier 9 at 9.960). No tie group straddles rank 15, so the top-15 set and the hub-drug counts do not depend on tie order. In the scenario table `top_pair` is the first of a tied top tier in row order and carries no information.
- **Potency.** Spearman rho between MLSS and the pair's mean pIC50: 0.9967 among the 77 cross pairs (R^2 0.994; the manuscript's 0.997), 0.6425 over all 153. Rank R^2 of the MLSS: class (cross versus same target) alone 0.756; potency alone 0.413; class plus potency 0.989.
- **What the weight analysis can see.** In the top 15, C = B = V = 1 and all 15 carry the identical Synergy 0.960357, so only P can reorder them. Port check (`fig6d_port_check_log.txt`): rho = 1.000000 for all 28 scenario pairs; ablation rank shifts 0, 0, 0, 3.6, 0 and score drops 0.013998, 0.007332, 0.001901, -0.017109, 0.960357, identical to the R log.

What the numbers say:
- **The correlation input does not change the order.** Replacing r by the raw, Kcnj11- or Ins1-normalized value, by the Gapdh-recalculated value, by nothing, or by any of the 120 observed values leaves the order of the 153 combinations and the top 15 unchanged (Spearman 1.000000 for the Gapdh, Kcnj11, Ins1 and raw rows and at least 0.99998 for the others, except the linear-scale treatment-adjusted row, 0.99992). Scores move (up to 0.38 for the raw scheme, 1.65 for the adjusted value) because only the 30 cross pairs with a bonus carry the term and the top pairs share the same multiplier, so they move together.
- **The sign of r does not change the order either.** The treatment-adjusted correlation of Cacna1d and Glp1r is negative: -0.691 on the linear scale (df 8, uncorrected p = 0.027, BH q = 0.33 over 136 pairs; no pair has q < 0.05) and -0.655 on the log scale (p = 0.040, q = 0.45), against +0.960 pooled and Hes1-normalized. A negative r turns the Synergy Bonus into an Antagonism Penalty (top score 8.31 instead of 9.96), yet Spearman is 0.9999 and the top 15 are the same. The order is the same whether the correlation term is a bonus or a penalty.
- **Gapdh handling does not change the order.** Under both recalculations Cacna1d and Glp1r remain hubs, the same 18 drugs enter, 30 scores change by at most 0.015 and no combination changes rank (the 76 same-target pairs are untouched). The consensus status of the Cacna1d-Glp1r edge under C1 does not matter to the MLSS.
- **The hub threshold changes the ranking.** Degree >= 6 leaves one hub (GLP1R): 7 drugs, 21 same-target combinations, no cross pair, none of the reported top 15. Degree >= 4 adds CACNA1C: 27 drugs on 3 targets, 351 combinations, Spearman 0.954 on the 153 shared combinations, 9 of the reported top 15 kept. The threshold was set after the result was seen (post hoc).
- **Potency and target mapping set the order.** With potency values shuffled across the drugs, agreement with the reported order falls to a mean of 0.755 and the reported top 15 are recovered in 2.9 of 15 on average, which is the chance value (15 x 15 / 77 = 2.9); no draw reached Spearman 0.95 and 0.2% kept 10 or more. With the target labels shuffled the mean is 0.322 and 7.4 of 15 are kept (7.8% of draws keep 10 or more; the best draw keeps all 15). In every draw the order inside the cross-target class still follows the potency values supplied (mean R^2 0.980, minimum 0.966): whatever potencies go in, the MLSS returns their order.
- **Weights.** Only C and P matter among the weights (B and V are constant in the cross class): dropping C or P lowers Spearman to 0.893 or 0.923; the top 15 survive all eight weight sets.

## 5. What the scenarios do not test
- Biological validity of any pair: no synergy, efficacy, safety or dose-response is measured or simulated; the score has no ground truth, so the stability above is a property of the arithmetic and not support for the weights or the pairs.
- Whether the pooled network reflects co-regulation (INS-1 is one preparation, 12 observations; that question is WP1 and WP2). The scenarios vary the inputs of the scoring function, not the experiment.
- The values in DrugCentral, their assay conditions, or other potency rules (median instead of maximum, non-human values allowed, one assay type only). The potency permutation moves the supplied values; it does not replace them.
- Other drug sources or the multi-gene matching rule: the 13 dropped dihydropyridines and any other drug universe were not scored. Only the hub threshold changes the universe here (>= 4 adds CACNA1C drugs).
- Drug-drug, pharmacokinetic or regulatory information, and the approval tiers of Fig. 6e/6f, which are annotations added after the ranking.
- Tie handling beyond what is reported: the top tier is a set of 9 (or more) tied pairs and is not ordered by the score.

## 6. Direction table (`direction_table.csv`)
One row per drug and per analyzed-gene target with a human DrugCentral record; `used_in_mlss` marks the 18 rows that enter the ranking. Fold changes are within-experiment group means over CTRL (matrix; agree with Additional file 3 to 0.0007), adjusted p from Additional file 3.
- GLP-1R agonists (hub drugs albiglutide, liraglutide; also exenatide, semaglutide, lixisenatide, dulaglutide, glucagon): *Glp1r* mRNA 0.58-fold with DAPT (adjusted *p* 0.0003) and 0.75-fold with DKK-1 (0.010). Agonism raises receptor signaling while the transcript is lower: the drug action opposes the change.
- L-type channel blockers (hub drugs isradipine, (S)-nitrendipine, nitrendipine; also eight more): *Cacna1d* mRNA 0.76-fold with DAPT (0.003) and 0.73-fold with DKK-1 (0.001). Inhibition points the same way as the lower transcript: it adds to the change. The DAPT value depends on the Gapdh reference: with all four wells it is 1.46-fold (adjusted *p* 1.7e-5) and the relation becomes "opposes"; with the mean of the two valid wells (C0) it stays at 0.76-fold and the relation is unchanged.
- The same drugs have human CACNA1C records (not a hub, not used): *Cacna1c* 0.71-fold (DAPT, 0.013) and 0.82-fold (DKK-1, 0.135, not supported).
- The mRNA level is a proxy for target abundance, not activity, so the labels are directional readings only. The MLSS never uses the action type, so a blocker plus an agonist is scored like any other cross-target pair. The Fig. 6f labels (Antagonist, Agonist) are typed into the script; DrugCentral gives "GATING INHIBITOR"/"BLOCKER" and "AGONIST". The Fig. 6f mapped genes Tcf7 and Ptbp1 for the GLP-1R agonists have no DrugCentral row, so no direction exists for them.

## 7. Where the pIC50 values come from (`pic50_sources.csv`)
"pIC50" is DrugCentral's ACT_VALUE (negative log10 molar) whatever the activity type, so it contains pKi and pEC50. Eleven of 18 drugs have a value:
- Types are mixed: 7 IC50, 2 EC50, 2 Ki. Sources: IUPHAR 5, scientific literature 3, WOMBAT-PK 2, ChEMBL 1.
- Calcium channel blockers: (S)-nitrendipine 8.4, nitrendipine 8.4, isradipine 9.3, nifedipine 7.7 are IC50 values on recombinant Cav1.3 in tsA cells (IUPHAR); amlodipine 7.0 is a Ki with no assay text.
- GLP-1R agonists: albiglutide 9.699 and liraglutide 10.2 are EC50 values (functional potency) with no assay text; exenatide 9.18 and lixisenatide 8.845 are IC50 values of binding displacement (CHOK1 or CHO cells with the human receptor); semaglutide 9.42 is an IC50 with no assay text; glucagon 7.0 is a Ki.
- Species: only human-target rows are used. Nine rows with values are excluded because the target is rat, mouse or macaque (for example isradipine 6.66 in rat against 9.3 in the human recombinant system; nimodipine 6.6 rat and nisoldipine 7.0 mouse would have supplied two of the seven missing values). Within the human rows the assay systems differ and five values have no assay description.
- The potency of the blockers (channel block) and of the agonists (functional or binding) is placed on one scale by P and by the multiplier; the classes are not measured by one assay type. With one eligible human record per drug and gene, taking the maximum had no effect here.

## 9. Manuscript sentences the results contradict or the code does not support
Quoted from `Submission_package_JTM/Manuscript_JTM.docx` (text read, file untouched).
**A. Contradicted or misleading**
1. "the MLSS was not recomputed." (Fig. 6 legend); "…so the MLSS would be expected to change little, but it was not recomputed." (Discussion; the expectation is now confirmed); Additional file 4, sheet Gapdh_sensitivity: "Permutation null and MLSS not recomputed." Superseded by section 8.
2. "With this threshold the drug-target step and the MLSS results are unchanged, because CACNA1D and GLP1R are still the only hub genes with annotated drugs." True against the earlier 20-gene run (its degree >= 6 hubs, Cacna1d, Glp1r, Hey1, Ptbp1, Tcf7l2, give the same two targets). On the 17-gene network the MLSS depends on the threshold: >= 6 leaves 7 GLP1R drugs, 21 combinations and none of the top 15; >= 4 gives 27 drugs on 3 targets and keeps 9 of the top 15 (Spearman 0.954).
3. "All 153 evaluated combinations consist of drugs with a single annotated target". The single target is produced by the code: all 11 CACNA1D drugs also have an exact human CACNA1C record (not a hub), and 13 further dihydropyridines (nicardipine, lercanidipine, benidipine, cilnidipine and nine others, listed in `mlss_facts.txt`) are absent because their L-type channel record is the multi-gene field "CACNA1D|CACNA1C", which the exact test does not match.
4. "A drug without a recorded pIC50 for its targets was assigned pIC50 = 0, which sets its potency term and the potency multiplier of the Synergy Bonus to zero". The multiplier is zero; the combination's P is not: in 56 of the 77 combinations with exactly one such drug P > 0, because P uses half the other drug's pIC50 (liraglutide + bepridil: P = 0.22).
5. "Top 5 stability was quantified as the overlap fraction between top-ranked combinations across schemes." The R code uses `rank(..., ties.method = "first") <= 5`; `rank` is ascending, so it compares the five lowest of the 15. For the ablation without P all 15 scores tie (one distinct value), the coded value is 0 and the value for the five largest is 1.0; the 0 is stored in Additional file 4, sheet Fig_6d_ablation_impact, column Top5_Stability. All other entries are 1 either way.
**B. Not supported by the code or the files**
6. "Combinations were ranked and stratified into four classes by score quartile (labelled Highest Confidence, Very High, High and Moderate Confidence in Additional file 4)…". No code produces these classes and Additional file 4 contains no such labels or column (searched every cell); with 47 distinct scores and 125 tied combinations quartile cuts would also fall inside tie groups.
7. "…and druggability status (Yes/Target/Potential) to identify hub genes". No druggability field exists in the Fig. 6b or MLSS code; hubs are chosen by degree only.
8. "Incretin Signaling (Glp1r, Tcf7), Wnt Signaling (Tcf7l2, Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt9a)" (Methods) and "incretin signaling (Glp1r, Tcf7), Wnt signaling (Tcf7l2, Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt9a)" (Results). `Fig_6b_Consensus_Hub_Network.r` and `Fig_6b_Gene_Centrality_Summary.csv` put Tcf7 in Wnt Signaling and only Glp1r in Incretin; the manuscript list follows the curated Fig. 6f assignment. Labels only, no effect on scores.
9. "These hubs were cross-referenced with DrugCentral 2023 [40] to identify approved drugs, with all pairwise combinations evaluated for network coverage by intersecting drug targets with the pooled consensus correlations." The code keeps every human DrugCentral row of a hub gene, approved or not, and reads the Hes1-normalized correlation of any gene pair, not only consensus pairs (the preceding Methods sentence says so).
10. "The network pharmacology framework relied on r ≥ 0.7 gene correlations, potentially excluding moderate-strength interactions". The MLSS code has no |r| cut; it keeps Hes1-normalized pairs with p < 0.05 (all 120, smallest r 0.752).
11. "the normalized mean binding affinity across targets". Per drug and gene the code takes the maximum over IC50, Ki and EC50 records of the human target, then the mean over a drug's hub targets, then the mean of the two drugs (section 7).
12. "in the data, 7 of 18 drugs have no recorded pIC50". Correct for human-target IC50/Ki/EC50 records only; two of the seven have non-human values (nimodipine, nisoldipine).
13. "Consensus pairs were defined as those with p ≤ 0.05 and the same sign of r under all four conditions". `Fig_4d_script.r` intersects the sets with p < 0.05 and has no sign test; none of the 21 pairs has discordant signs, so the numbers do not change.
14. "assessing equitable target distribution across functional modules". B counts target genes per drug; modules play no part.
