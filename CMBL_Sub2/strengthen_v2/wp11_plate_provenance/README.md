## 2. What was done
* **Exports of 8 Nov** (3 plates x threshold as found / 150 RFU). `check_threshold150.py` compares the blocks of the sheet (WP1 `ins1_sheet_cq.csv`) with the Cq of each export, sorted block against sorted block (tolerance 0.005 cycles), for the target and the Gapdh wells, with Gr1, Gr2, Gr1 plus Gr2 and plate 2 as candidate sources.
* **Threshold reconstruction.** `reconstruct_threshold.py` recomputes Cq from the baseline-subtracted amplification curves by linear interpolation of the first crossing of a threshold T. Validation: it returns the known thresholds of the user's exports (150.000, 138.060, 36.070) and reproduces the exported Cq to 0.0001 cycles. On plate 1 it finds the threshold of the 2025 re-export (45.700) and the threshold that best reproduces the sheet (209.91 RFU, mean absolute difference 0.040 cycles over 92 values; Gapdh blocks alone 211.6, target blocks alone 209.5; Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt9a, Lef1, Tcf7 alone 209.91, 0.029). Largest block differences at 209.91: 0.02 to 0.12 cycles for the analyzed genes and Gapdh; Wnt2b (not analyzed) differs by 0.77 in its CTRL block and has one failed well (G04, peak 89.6 RFU) in the DAPT block.
* **Correction made during this work package (8 Nov).** A first version of the fit required that every well of the plate crossed the threshold, which capped T at 89.6 RFU because of that one failed Wnt2b well and produced the wrong statement that no single threshold could reproduce the sheet. The script now skips blocks with a non-crossing well and says so; the conclusion above is from the corrected run.
* **Group-level comparison** (`check_refA_vs_plate1.py`): group means of the four wells, so that no well mapping is needed (the 24 June rows carry no well labels).
* **26 August and 9 November** (`check_aug26_nov09_order.py`): plate-order and well-label checks of the instrument export, the user's workbook `...Ptbp1 -  Quantification Summary.xlsx` (CFX columns B to H, calculation columns J to O) and the matrix rows. Sorted blocks, well/Cq agreement, and a descriptive comparison of target-to-Gapdh pairings.
* **STRING** (`../wp10_versions/string_web_export_check.py`).

## 3. Effect of the 26 August sort on the analysis (descriptive, not a new analysis)
The matrix pairs the k-th smallest target Cq with the k-th smallest Gapdh Cq of the same group, so "replicate k" of this run is a rank, not a well (fix list A13). The group means do not depend on the pairing. What the pairing changes is the spread inside a group:
| 26 August, five genes | within-group SD of dCq | mean residual correlation between the genes | mean (DAPT - CTRL) dCq |
|---|---|---|---|
| as analyzed (sheet vectors, rank pairing, DAPT Gapdh A10 and B10 replaced) | 0.132 | +0.536 | +0.662 |
| instrument values, rank pairing (all four Gapdh wells) | 0.397 | +0.975 | -0.276 |
| instrument values, pairing by plate order (all four Gapdh wells) | 0.481 | +0.938 | -0.276 |
Residual correlation = Pearson r of the values after removing group means, averaged over the 10 gene pairs. Reading: (i) the within-run correlation of +0.536 that WP1 found for this run is the value of the matrix as analyzed; (ii) the sort-based pairing makes the replicate spread small (SD 0.13 against 0.40 to 0.48), and the plate-order pairing is only a plausible alternative because the sheet does not say which well holds which cDNA; (iii) replacing the two Gapdh wells shifts the DAPT group by about 0.94 cycles for all five genes together, which is why the mean DAPT-minus-CTRL dCq is +0.662 with the replacement and -0.276 without. The Methods already disclose the replacement and its sensitivity (10 of the 21 consensus pairs lost, 10 new). None of this was used to change the reported analysis.
